# Medium specificity: the practice in the artist's own words

Asked in October 2026 what he is doing, Liu answered: he approaches artificial-intelligence art along the line of
media art's question of **medium specificity**, and asks how to go beyond simply prompting. This page collects his own
statements on the ideas behind the work, so that questions about "why" can be answered from them.

## Complex systems and operational images

In October 2026 Liu asked that his About text lead with these two terms: the **aesthetics of complex systems** (images
that come out of feedback loops, recursion and long technical pipelines rather than from a single intention, with
orders nobody designed, like Turing patterns) and **operational images** (images made by and for machines: dataset
captions, scores that rank a face, tokens that decide what a model can see). Geopolitics follows from there.
(Source: the artist's instruction for the About page, 25 October 2026; data/site.json about.text.)

## Beyond the prompt

"When people talk about AI art, they often argue about who the author is: the person who writes the prompt, or the
machine? We come at it from a completely different angle and make works without prompts." (translated from Liu's
statement on *Steering Through the Inner Residue*, 2026)

The reference is Alvin Lucier's *I Am Sitting in a Room* (1969): record a sentence, play it back and record it again,
over and over, until the words disappear and only the resonance of the room remains, and every room has its own
voiceprint. "Media art has always asked about medium specificity: what is unique to each medium? So what is the
medium specificity of AI image generation?" Without a prompt, the diffusion model's output is fed straight back into
its input; the small differences of each reproduction accumulate into different images. Colours drift into
oversaturation, forms break into patterns, and every model has its own visual fingerprint: an aesthetic nobody
designed, grown by the system from its own algorithmic structure, like Turing patterns.
→ [Steering Through the Inner Residue](../works/steering-through-the-inner-residue.md)

## From the medium outward

For Liu, asking about the nature of a medium is what media artists should do anyway; with AI the question should then
extend outward, to **geopolitics, data politics and the "operational image"** (images made by and for machines rather
than for human viewers). → [The material infrastructure of AI](material-infrastructure-of-ai.md)

## Feedback, recursion, cybernetics

Feedback and recursion are part of his artistic strategy: like Lucier's room, recursion is a way to explore latent
spaces and embedding spaces. His teaching also follows a line of thinking about **cybernetics**.
→ [Recursion and feedback](recursion-and-feedback.md)

## Why local AI

The main reasons are set out in his thesis ([Heat as (Generative) Image Making](../texts/heat-as-generative-image-making.md)).
Data ethics is part of it, but more important for him: using small, lightweight AI gives people better choices in
teaching; running models locally allows more variation and more ways to operate them; and the aesthetics are not
held by cloud companies. → [Workshops](workshops.md)

## Taiwan

Taiwan and the semiconductor supply chain inform his context as a Taiwanese media artist: how he imagines materials,
technology and capital. → [The material infrastructure of AI](material-infrastructure-of-ai.md)

## Will AI replace artists?

No. For Liu, artists use AI in order to interact with the technical objects around them.
