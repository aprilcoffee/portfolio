# Seminar reading list: Seeing Like a Machine and critical AI

The texts Ting-Chun Liu reads with students in his seminar *Seeing Like a Machine* (Bauhaus University Weimar,
winter semester 2026/27) and a wider shelf of critical AI readings he uses in teaching (list from the artist,
October 2026). For the chat: when a visitor asks what to read, pick one or two that fit the question and say in a
sentence why; never list them all. Short notes below say what each text is about where that is well established;
where only the title is known, only the title is given.

## Operational images (the seminar's core)

- **Harun Farocki, "Phantom Images" (2004).** Farocki's essay on images made not to be seen by people but to operate
  inside machines (missile cameras, surveillance, industrial vision): the starting point of the idea of the
  operational (operative) image.
- **Harun Farocki, "Bilderschatz" (2001).** Farocki's idea of a "treasury" or archive of film images, sorted by what
  they show and do; useful for thinking about sorting footage by meaning.
- **Aud Sissel Hoel, "Operative Images" (2018).** Takes Farocki's term into media theory: images as tools that act,
  not only representations.
- **Trevor Paglen, "Invisible Images" (2019 version).** On machine-to-machine images: most images are now made and read
  by machines, and that vision serves power.
- **Jussi Parikka, *Operational Images* (2023)**, including the chapter on images between light and data. A book on
  operational images from the visual to the "invisual": data, measurement, logistics, climate.
- **Jan Distelmeyer, "Which Operativity?" (2023).** Asks what "operative" actually means when the term is applied to
  images and computing.

## Machines describing and sorting images

- **Leonardo Impett and Fabian Offert, "There Is a Digital Art History" (2022).** On what large vision models like
  CLIP mean for art history, and why their way of seeing has to be studied critically.
- **Daniel Chávez Heras, "Computational Ekphrasis" (2026).** On machines that turn images into text (captioning) as a
  form of ekphrasis.
- **Adam Harvey and Jules LaPlace, "Researchers Gone Wild" (2021).** On face and image datasets scraped from the web
  "in the wild" and where they end up.
- **Hito Steyerl, "Mean Images", *New Left Review* 140/141 (2023).** Images made by machine learning are statistical
  renderings rather than pictures of existing things: they trade photographic indexicality for probability, converge on
  the average like Galton's composites, and so are "mean" in both senses. Steyerl shows what Stable Diffusion makes of
  "an image of hito steyerl".
- **Hito Steyerl, "The Hardest Part" (2025).**

## Data, bias and power

- **Wendy Hui Kyong Chun, "Queerying Homophily" (2018).** How network science's "birds of a feather" assumption
  (homophily) builds segregation into recommendation systems.
- **Nick Seaver, "Care and Scale: Decorrelative Ethics in Algorithmic Recommendation" (2021).**
- **Joy Buolamwini and Timnit Gebru, "Gender Shades" (2018).** Intersectional accuracy gaps in commercial face
  analysis: worst for darker-skinned women.
- **Catherine D'Ignazio and Lauren F. Klein, *Data Feminism* (2020)**, including the chapter "Show Your Work" on the
  invisible labour behind data.
- **Johanna T. Wallenborn, "(Un)dressed: How AI Reinforces Regressive Gender Politics"**, in *un/learn ai*, the
  KITeGG publication series, vol. 2 (the series in which Ting-Chun's own text appears in vol. 3).
- **Kate Crawford, *Atlas of AI* (2021).** AI as an extractive industry: minerals, labour, data, classification.
- **James Bridle, *New Dark Age* (2018).** How more data and computation can make the world harder, not easier, to
  understand.
- **Dominik Vrabič Dežman (2025), on images of artificial intelligence in public media.** How AI is pictured (robots,
  blue brains) and what those stock images do.
- **Francis Hunger, "Unhype Artificial 'Intelligence'! A proposal to replace the deceiving terminology of AI"**,
  *Training the Archive* Working Paper 6. Argues that words like "intelligence" and "learning" mislead, and proposes
  more accurate terms for what these systems do.
- **Dzennifer Zachlod, "I Will Rather Lose My Job to AI Than My Dad"**, in *un/learn ai* (KITeGG), vol. 2.
- **Sarah Ciston, "ladymouth: Anti-Social-Media Art As Research"** (issue 15). ladymouth is a bot that answers posts
  in misogynist online forums (such as Reddit's Red Pill) with feminist quotes; the text treats the bot and the
  reactions it provokes as artistic research.
- **Fabian Offert and Ranjodh Singh Dhaliwal, "The Method of Critical AI Studies, A Propaedeutic".**
