# The material infrastructure of AI

AI looks immaterial but runs on graphics cards, electricity and semiconductor supply chains, many of
which lead back to Taiwan. Liu's work makes this visible through heat: computation's waste heat as
image material, and the phone warming in your hand. The perspective is explicitly that of a Taiwanese
artist, limited in computing power yet connected to the infrastructure global AI depends on.

## Works and research

- [Latent Heat Generation](../works/latent-heat-generation.md): thermal feedback between GPUs and Stable Diffusion; research notes at https://aprilcoffee.github.io/heat_as_image/
- [Sun](../works/sun.md) (2020): an early piece in which a web page heats the phone.

## What the thesis adds

The longest treatment is the Materiality chapter of [Heat as (Generative) Image Making](../texts/heat-as-generative-image-making.md). Its chain runs from the code that makes an image to the chip and back: an RTX 4090 draws about 450 W and is built by TSMC at 4 nm; transistor-grade quartz comes from a single place, Spruce Pine, North Carolina; wafers are made by three suppliers (Shin-Etsu Handotai, SUMCO, Siltronic) in cleanrooms; EUV lithography comes from one company, ASML, with mirrors from ZEISS; TSMC, founded in 1987 as a political project, makes about 90% of advanced chips (2022). CUDA ties most AI software to NVIDIA cards. The thesis closes the chain in Taiwan: the 2021 drought, the "Silicon Shield", and the paradox that the chips that protect Taiwan also power systems that undermine others, with no guarantee that Taiwan itself will not become expendable.

Other texts and talks on the same ground: [On the Materiality of Artificial Intelligence](../texts/on-the-materiality-of-artificial-intelligence.md) (*un/learn ai*, 2025; Correlations, December 2024) and the 38C3 talk *arafed futures*, which sets embedding spaces against the semiconductor supply chain (https://media.ccc.de/v/38c3-arafed-futures-an-artist-dialogue-on-chip-storage-and-ai-accelerationism).
