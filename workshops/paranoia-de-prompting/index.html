Workshop

Paranoia De-Prompting

With
Leon-Etienne Kühr

Play with CLIP, the model that decides which picture matches which words, and watch its idea of “happy” or “nurse” slowly appear.

CLIP places images and texts in the same space, so a picture and a sentence can be compared with a single score. It sits inside many image generators and image search tools, and quietly decides what counts as a “match”.

Matching

Participants give CLIP an image and a few short texts, and it says which text fits best: a painting of a pipe against “this is a pipe” and “this isn’t a pipe”; a cat against “cat”, “Katze” and “gato”; a generated face against four emojis. The scores are rarely neutral, and the surprises are the point.

Searching

Then CLIP searches. A loop pulls random faces from a website that generates people who do not exist, scores each one against a word such as “happy”, and keeps the highest and lowest. Repeat it with “nurse”, “programmer” or “dumplings”, and the machine’s idea of each word slowly appears. With food it gets stranger: in one round, a plate of soup with a sausage scored higher for “happy” than a golden schnitzel.

Your own words

Participants repeat the experiments in a notebook with a simple interface, with their own words and images.
