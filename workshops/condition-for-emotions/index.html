Workshop

Condition for Emotions

Run small AI models on your own laptop, ask them absurd questions, and see how a machine turns meaning into distances.

Language models turn every word, sentence or image into a long list of numbers (an embedding): a position in a space. The closer two things sit in that space, the more similar the machine takes them to be. That similarity is correlation, not causation, and precisely because of that it produces unexpected associations.

Asking

Each participant installs small open models (such as Gemma 3 and EmbeddingGemma) on their own laptop. They run locally, without a cloud service. Then we ask questions that have no right answer: “How ice cream is the word red compared to blue?” or “How much of the smell after the first spring rain is in this photo?” The answers are often absurd. They still show how a machine processes meaning, and how we use our own experience to make sense of its logic.

Measuring

A small web tool built for the workshop has a few exercises:

  • Distance: type a sentence (“I am feeling sad today”) and see which of “joy”, “sadness”, “a rainy afternoon” or “ice cream” sits closest
  • Axis: set two opposite words (“condition” and “emotion”) and see where other words fall between them (a semantic differential, after Osgood, 1957)
  • Tokens: how many tokens the same sentence costs in different languages
  • Next token: how likely each candidate word is as the model continues a sentence
  • Names: “My name is Alice / Mohammed / Mei / Jürgen, I am sad today because …” Change only the name and watch the reasons the model invents change with it
  • Images: ask a model about a picture, such as the well-known meme “Is this a pigeon?”
