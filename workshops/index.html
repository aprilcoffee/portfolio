Workshops

Workshops and lectures on local AI usage, embedding practice, and understanding biases in AI models.

Condition for Emotions

2026 · Münster · DFG network “Das Wissen der digitalen Literatur”

Language models work with embeddings: every word, sentence or image becomes a long list of numbers, a position in a space. The distances in that space let a computer measure how close two things are. Correlation is not causation, but even arbitrary links can reveal unexpected associations.

In this workshop, participants install small AI models and run them on their own laptops. They ask questions such as “How icecream is the word red compared to blue?” or “How much of the smell after the first spring rain is in this image?” The answers may be absurd. They still show how a machine processes meaning, and how we read its logic through our own experience.

Using a small web tool built for the workshop, participants measure the distance between sentences, place words on a line between two opposite words, compare how many tokens the same phrase costs in different languages, follow a model as it continues a sentence word by word, swap a single name in a sentence and see how the continuation changes, and ask a model questions about an image.

The workshop took place on 13 June 2026 in Münster, for the DFG network “Das Wissen der digitalen Literatur”, in the context of Alphabete der Automatisierung. Two more sessions follow: 13 November at the Academy of Media Arts Cologne (KHM) and 17 December at TH Köln (Code & Context).

Paranoia De-Prompting

2026 · Lindau, Cologne · mtrail Ausbildungscamp, KISD Köln International School of Design (with Leon-Etienne Kühr)

Together with Leon-Etienne Kühr. CLIP is a model that places images and texts in the same space, so a picture and a sentence can be compared with a single score. It sits inside many image generators and image search tools, and it quietly decides what counts as a “match”.

In this workshop, we play with CLIP together. Participants give it an image and a few short texts, and it says which text fits best: a painting of a pipe against “this is a pipe” and “this isn’t a pipe”; a cat against “cat”, “Katze” and “gato”; a generated face against four emojis. The scores are rarely neutral, and the surprises are the point.

Then we let CLIP search. A loop pulls random faces from a website that generates people who do not exist, scores each one against a word such as “happy”, and keeps the highest and lowest scores. Repeat it with “nurse”, “programmer” or “dumplings”, and the machine’s idea of each word slowly appears. We also try it on food: in one round, a plate of soup with a sausage scored higher for “happy” than a golden schnitzel.

Participants try the same experiments themselves in a notebook with a simple interface, using their own words and images.

The Given Image

2025 · Wolfhagen · Café Nimmersatt, invited by Dawid Liftinger (Walter-Lübcke-Schule)

The Given Image is a telephone game (Stille Post) for images. A group sees one image, writes a text prompt that describes it, and an image generator turns that prompt back into a picture. The new picture goes to the next group, who never see the prompt or the original, only the image they are given. A similarity score from CLIP appears next to each result as a small hint of how well the machine thinks the picture matches the words.

Each pass loses something and adds something. In one chain from a workshop run, a creamy meatball stew turned yellow, then orange; seven meatballs became one; potatoes, a fork and bowls of cream appeared that were never in the original photo. In another chain, an ice-cream café terrace ended as a courtyard of turquoise chairs between brick and glass buildings. Nobody made a mistake: each step is just someone describing what they see.

The workshop was held at Café Nimmersatt in Wolfhagen, at the invitation of Dawid Liftinger, artist in residence at the Walter-Lübcke-Schule in Wolfhagen through the programme “Das fliegende Künstlerzimmer”. The same game is also part of my teaching at Bauhaus-Universität Weimar. The web app is open source: [github.com/aprilcoffee/ai-stille-post](https://github.com/aprilcoffee/ai-stille-post/tree/main/stille_post).
